**Abstract**: While the Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program aims to enable lowincome households to access high-opportunity neighborhoods, landlord discrimination in the private rental market systematically impedes such upward mobility. Using 2015-2023 American Housing Survey data across 15 MSAs, this study develops the empirical concept of the counterfactual rent gap to measure the cost of landlord discrimination. Through propensity score matching, we find that HCV tenants pay an average of 46.07% less in out-of-pocket rent than similar unsubsidized market renters. Through a bounding framework leveraging leaser-in-place HCV tenants and top-income tenants under Small Area Fair Market Rents (SAFMR), we isolate the neighborhood opportunity loss within the range of 23.48% to 45.65%, which accounts for 51.0% to 99.1% of the rent gap. Furthermore, our policy evaluation shows no significant effects of source of income (SOI) nondiscrimination protection laws on the neighborhood opportunity loss, which is likely due to weak enforcement. Meanwhile, SAFMR yields heterogeneous outcomes, effectively narrowing neighborhood opportunity loss in slack housing markets while widening the loss in tight markets. Policymakers should therefore prioritize proactive enforcement of SOI laws, the reduction of administrative burdens, and subsidy designs tailored to local market contexts.
